# Portraits: life reconstructions

Owner: portrait artist. One file per taxon: `public/portraits/<SpeciesId>.jpg`, for every id in
`SPECIES_IDS` (`src/data/types.ts`). Served by Vite from `public/`, so the runtime URL is
`/portraits/<SpeciesId>.jpg` (same origin, allowed by `img-src 'self'`).

These are AI-generated paleoart impressions. They are not forensic reconstructions built on a scan
of the fossil, and they do not match the procedural 3D skeletons measurement for measurement.

## Required on-screen caption

Show this caption, word for word, everywhere a portrait appears (cards, panel, modal), including the
chimpanzee:

> AI-generated artistic impression. Skin, hair and eye colour are unknown for extinct species.

Suggested extra line for the two taxa whose faces are mostly guesswork:

- `denisovans`: "Speculative: face based on a single skull (Harbin, China)."
- `homo-luzonensis`: "Speculative: no skull of this species has been found."

## How the files were made

- 17 JPEGs, 768 x 768 px, JPEG quality 82, 98-153 KB each.
- Generated at 1024 x 1024 with the `generate_image` tool (aspect ratio 1:1), then converted with
  `sips -s format jpeg -s formatOptions 82 -Z 768 <in> --out public/portraits/<id>.jpg`.
- Every prompt has the form `<taxon description> Style: <shared style>.` The shared style is:

> photorealistic museum paleoart life reconstruction, head-and-shoulders portrait, three-quarter view, calm neutral expression, soft directional studio light like a natural history museum diorama, plain very dark slate-blue background (#0F172A), sharp focus on the face, realistic skin pores, hair and eyes, no text, no logos, no jewelry unless specified, no gore, square 1:1 composition

- The prompts in the table were copied from the image tool's own log of what it used. They are the
  exact text sent, not a retyped version.
- After the first batch drifted toward modern human faces, the prompts not yet generated (boisei,
  robustus, habilis, naledi, floresiensis, luzonensis, denisovans) were made stricter before their
  first attempt.

## Weak portraits

These four still look too much like modern people after their one retry:

1. `homo-floresiensis`: reads as a modern woman. The brow ridge, the forward-jutting lower face and the
   very small braincase barely show, and her tiny size cannot be seen in a close-up.
2. `homo-luzonensis`: reads as a modern woman. No skull exists, so any face is a guess. Consider a
   silhouette instead, or at least the "speculative" line above.
3. `homo-naledi`: face too modern and the small head does not come across. The generator also added a
   dark hide top that nothing in the fossil or archaeological record supports.
4. `homo-erectus`: the retry gave a stronger brow ridge, but the forehead is still fairly upright and
   the expression a little stern. There is a sculpted-bust cut edge at the lower right.

Why this matters: when an extinct species from Africa or Island Southeast Asia is shown with a
generic modern face, viewers can read it as saying that people living there today look like extinct
hominins. That is the stereotype this project has to avoid. Replace these four before they are shown
to kids, either with a paleoartist's reconstruction or with another generation pass that edits from
